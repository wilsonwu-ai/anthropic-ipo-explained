 We have a novel capital and corporate governance structure. We are incorporated as a public benefit corporation under Delaware law, a structure consistent with our 
 commitment to artificial intelligence (“AI”) safety and responsibility. As a public benefit corporation, we are required to balance the pecuniary interests of our 
 stockholders, the best interests of those materially affected by our conduct, and the specific public benefit purpose identified in our amended and restated certificate of 
 incorporation: the responsible development and maintenance of advanced AI for the long-term benefit of humanity. We created our Long-Term Benefit Trust (the 
 “LTBT”) to help ensure that Anthropic, PBC is managed in a manner that responsibly balances its goals as a public benefit corporation. The LTBT is intended to 
 operate as an independent body of financially disinterested trustees with expertise in AI safety, national security, public policy, and social enterprise. Through a special 
 class of common stock, Class T common stock, the LTBT generally has the authority to elect and remove the Class T directors, who together will constitute a majority 
 of the authorized members of our board of directors. Our Class T common stock does not otherwise have voting rights with respect to matters submitted to 
 stockholders generally and has no economic rights. Immediately prior to the completion of this offering, we will also issue a single share of Class F common stock, a 
 special class of our common stock, to a limited liability company organized by our co-founders (the “Founder LLC”). Dario Amodei, our Chief Executive Officer and a 
 member of our board of directors, and Daniela Amodei, our President and Chairperson of our board of directors, together with our five other co-founders, will initially 
 be the sole members of the Founder LLC. Our Class F common stock, which will be held by the Founder LLC, will be entitled to an aggregate number of votes that 
 equals 50.1% of the total voting power of our outstanding shares of capital stock with respect to matters submitted to stockholders generally, including the election of 
 our directors other than the Class T directors. Our Class F common stock will not have any economic rights. Immediately prior to the completion of this offering, we 
 will also authorize a special class of our common stock, Class E common stock, which will be issued to our employees, excluding our co-founders. Our Class E common 
 stock will be entitled to vote only on certain limited matters and will not have any economic rights. In addition, we have authorized Class A common stock and Class N 
 common stock. The rights of the holders of Class A common stock and Class N common stock are identical, except with respect to voting and conversion. Each share of 
 Class A common stock is entitled to one vote and shares of Class N common stock have no voting rights, except as otherwise required by law. The holders of our Class 
 N common stock are primarily strategic business partners, and shares of Class N common stock will generally convert to Class A common stock upon a sale or transfer 
 to an unaffiliated third party. Our Class A common stock is not convertible. See the sections titled “Risk Factors—Risks Related to our Governance Structure,” “Our 
 Governance Structure and Its Goals,” and “Description of Capital Stock” for more information. 
 We are considered a “controlled company” within the meaning of the Nasdaq Global Select Market corporate governance requirements. See the sections titled “Our 
 Governance Structure and Its Goals,” “Management,” and “Description of Capital Stock” for additional information.

 Select Defined Terms 
 The following are abbreviations, acronyms, and definitions of certain terms used in this prospectus: 
 “ 
 accelerators 
 ” refer to processors purpose-built or adapted for the large-scale computations required to pre- 
 train, post-train, refine, and run super intelligence models. These processors are significantly more efficient and 
 faster than general-purpose processors for these workloads. 
 “ 
 agent loop 
 ” refers to the iterative cycle in which an SI agent receives a task, takes an action, such as planning 
 a project, running code, or calling a tool, observes the result, and decides its next step, with this cycle repeating 
 until the task is complete. The agent loop is the core execution pattern underlying agentic SI behavior. 
 “ 
 agentic AI 
 ” or “ 
 agentic SI 
 ” refers to systems that, with varying degrees of autonomy, can plan, determine, 
 and sequence actions and execute them to accomplish defined objectives, including through interaction with 
 external tools, services, datasets, and environments on behalf of a user. 
 “ 
 API 
 ” refers to application programming interface, a standardized interface that allows external software to 
 access a system’s capabilities programmatically. SI model providers may offer APIs that allow software 
 developers to send inputs to a model and receive outputs, enabling integration into the software developers’ 
 products and workflows. 
 “ 
 Applied AI 
 ” refers to a discipline and organizational function focused on embedding SI capabilities directly 
 into production business environments to address specific customer requirements. Applied AI teams, which 
 include forward-deployed engineers, work with enterprises to design and deploy custom solutions, specialized 
 workflows, and domain-specific implementations tailored to a customer’s own systems and processes. 
 “ 
 artificial intelligence 
 ” or “ 
 AI 
 ” refers to a machine-based system that learns or is trained from data and, for 
 explicit or implicit objectives and with varying levels of autonomy, processes that data to infer patterns, 
 generate outputs—such as predictions, content, recommendations, and decisions—and act upon physical or 
 virtual environments. For purposes of this prospectus, we use the terms “artificial intelligence,” “AI,” “super 
 intelligence,” and “SI” interchangeably. 
 “ 
 closing the loop 
 ” refers to a development cycle in which successive versions of an SI model assist in building 
 the next, with the goal of compounding capability gains over time. 
 “ 
 compute 
 ” refers to the computational processing resources used to train and run SI models, including, 
 without limitation, specialized accelerators, memory, networking, storage, and power systems that are deployed 
 in data centers or other physical computing environments. 
 “ 
 compute multipliers 
 ” refer to algorithmic and methodological improvements in training and inference that 
 increase the effective output of a given amount of compute. 
 “ 
 computer use 
 ” refers to an SI capability that allows a model to observe and interact with a graphical user 
 interface, such as clicking, typing, and navigating applications, similar to how a human operator would. 
 “ 
 Constitutional AI 
 ” refers to an approach to training AI models using a set of written principles, which we 
 refer to as Claude’s constitution, currently organized around four priorities: being broadly safe, broadly ethical, 
 compliant with our guidelines, and genuinely helpful. Under Constitutional AI, our models evaluate, critique, 
 and revise their own responses against these principles, enabling us to define and control model behavior 
 through auditable principles, rather than relying solely on fixed rule-based instructions or human reviewers. 
 “ 
 context window 
 ” refers to the maximum amount of text, code, or other content, measured in tokens, that an 
 SI model can process in a given request, encompassing instructions, prior conversation history, user input, and 
 the model’s output. 
 “ 
 CSP 
 ” refers to a cloud service provider, a technology company that owns and operates large-scale cloud 
 computing infrastructure and provides on-demand compute, storage, and networking. CSPs may also serve as 
 distribution channels through which SI products and services are made available to customers. The largest
